# RaCo rotation keypoints — short explainer

A keypoint detector names pixels it can find again in another picture of the same scene. Repeatability is the fraction of those pixels that land within a few pixels of a detection in the other picture, after the known warp is applied. If you rotate the photograph in the plane and the detector was not built or trained for that, the second list often names different pixels and the fraction collapses.

RaCo, from Shenoi, Lindenberger, Sarlin, and Pollefeys ([arXiv:2602.15755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15755), 3DV 2026), is a small network in the ALIKED-N16 family. It emits a detection heatmap, a ranking score, and a 2×2 covariance. The paper's rotation result is not an equivariant convolution. Section 3.2 says training pairs with rotations over the full circle are enough, and that adding equivariant convolutions gains about 3 points of repeatability while making inference about 10× slower. The abstract says the same thing. A bookmarked post calls the detector rotationally equivariant. This demo treats that as a claim about repeatability under rotation, and measures that.

VisMatch 1.4.0 ([gmberton/vismatch](https://github.com/gmberton/vismatch/releases/tag/v1.4.0)) wraps the detector as `raco-lightglue`: RaCo keypoints, an ALIKED descriptor, and a LightGlue matcher trained on that pair. The wrapper turns the ranker and the covariance head off, and its comment says the keypoints stay the same. The same install's `aliked-lightglue` is ALIKED-N16, the learned detector without that full-circle augmentation. `aliked-n16rot` exists in LightGlue and is not the one VisMatch builds by default, so it is not the baseline.

The protocol follows Section 4.3 in miniature. Take a HPatches reference photo. Cut the largest center square that still sits inside the photo after any in-plane turn. Rotate about that square's center, resize to 512, and add Gaussian noise with σ = 10 so interpolation is not a free match. Keep 200 keypoints on each view. A point counts when it and its warp are at least 3 px inside the canvas and the other view has a detection within 3 px. The score is the symmetric fraction. The homography is known because the rotation is synthetic. The dataset's own homographies are not used.

The pre-registered bar, fixed before the measured run, has two parts. Over 30° to 150°, RaCo's mean repeatability has to beat ALIKED by at least 0.10. And RaCo's own number at 0° can fall by at most 0.10 at its worst angle. Both have to hold. One of them is a partial. Neither is a fail. Seeds 0, 1, and 2 each pick four of the first 20 HPatches reference frames. In the sequences archive those twenty are all illumination scenes, because those folder names sort first.

On this 4-vCPU run the status is **pass**. The gap over 30–150° is 0.677 (RaCo 0.820, ALIKED 0.143). The drop from 0.889 at 0° to 0.802 at 135° is 0.087. ALIKED is still usable near 0° (0.780) and is about 0.03 at 90°. At 2 px, which is the threshold in the paper's Figure 5, RaCo stays near 0.80. That is the same shape as the paper's curve. It is not their rotation AUC: this is four images, 15° steps, and 0–180°, not twenty images at 10° through 360°.

The rotation result does not extend to every stress. On one image, blurring the rotated view with σ = 3 drops RaCo to 0.21. A 64 px canvas reverses the order (ALIKED 0.93, RaCo 0.79). Halving the scale at 45° drops RaCo to 0.43. A budget of 16 points drops it to 0.38, and the ranker that the paper uses for small budgets is off in the VisMatch wrapper. A checkerboard smoke at 45° also has ALIKED ahead. That smoke was not a gate, and the bars were not moved after seeing it.
